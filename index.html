A backyard for a town of 6,000.

Carbondale sits at the foot of Mt. Sopris, where the Roaring Fork and Crystal Rivers meet. Red Hill rises above the north side of town — close enough to walk to from Main Street, big enough to spend a whole day exploring. It belongs to all of us.

§ 02

What we do, and why.

A short note from the Council

Red Hill is one of those rare places where a community took its public land seriously, and the land repaid the favor. What started in 1996 as a conversation between local citizens, the Town of Carbondale, and the BLM became — somehow, slowly, and with a lot of volunteer hours — a Special Recreation Management Area with a real trail system on it.

The Council exists to keep that going. We plan and build new trails in coordination with the BLM, maintain the existing network, manage seasonal closures that protect wintering wildlife, and try to be good stewards of an area that's increasingly loved (and increasingly busy).

Great trails are a public good. The people who use them should help take care of them — and we make that easy. — Red Hill Council Mission, 2000

We're a small board, an even smaller crew of regular volunteers, and a whole lot of generous neighbors. If you've ridden, hiked, or run Red Hill, you've already met us — you just didn't know it.

⚠ Seasonal Notice

Northside & Elk Traverse close every winter.

The Northside Loop, Elk Traverse, and all trails north of them are closed annually from December 1 through April 15 to protect wintering big-game habitat. Closures are signed at the trailhead.

☉ Mud Season

Stay off muddy trails — please.

During spring thaw and after heavy rain, the soil is at its most vulnerable. A single rutted ride causes weeks of damage and erosion. If your tires (or boots) are leaving prints, turn around and come back tomorrow.

§ 05 — About Us

A quarter-century of community stewardship.

From a 1996 meeting at the Town Hall to a nationally-recognized recreation area on the doorstep of Carbondale.

1996

The first conversation

Local citizens meet with the Town of Carbondale, the BLM, and area residents about the future of the 3,000 acres immediately north of town.

2000

Incorporated as a nonprofit

The Red Hill Council formally incorporates as a private 501(c)(3) — making the partnership with BLM official.

[PLAC.]

BLM designates Red Hill an SRMA

Red Hill is given Special Recreation Management Area status — a formal designation that protects the area for long-term public use.

Today

~75,000 visitors a year

A network of 12 named trails, managed in partnership with BLM, RFMBA, RFOV, and the Town of Carbondale — and maintained almost entirely by volunteers.

How we're organized
